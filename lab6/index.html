Design Description

Why these visual channels

I map GDP amount to rectangle area because a treemap is meant for parts of a whole. After summing leaf GDP, the United States and China occupy the largest cells, and Kenya is a sliver, so magnitude is visible without reading every number. GDP status has three classes, so I map it to fill from d3.schemeTableau10: blue for Increase, orange for Unchanged, and red for Decrease. Color does not compete with area, and a small cell can still show status. Continent and area do not get a second color, which would clash with the status legend. Nesting, padding, continent labels, and area outlines carry membership. Tooltips give country, continent, area, GDP, and status when a label cannot fit.

How the two segmentation strategies change the hierarchy

The two methods keep the same hierarchy and the same GDP values. Only the cut rule changes. Squarify keeps rectangles close to squares. Large economies are easier to compare by area, and country names fit more often, but continent groups look like packed blocks rather than clear bands. SliceDice alternates horizontal and vertical cuts by depth. Continents become stacked bands and areas become strips inside those bands, so the path from continent to area to country is easier to trace. The cost is long thin cells for small countries, which are hard to compare by area and often lose labels. Zooming into a parent then uses the full frame for one branch.
