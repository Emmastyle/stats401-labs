Design Description

Both treemaps map GDP to rectangle area because area is the channel D3 treemaps compute from .sum(), and it supports a part-to-whole reading: the United States and China occupy the largest cells, while Kenya is a thin remainder. GDP status is mapped to color so the three categories stay categorical and do not compete with area. The fills come from d3.schemeTableau10: blue for Increase, orange for Unchanged, and red for Decrease. Continent and area are shown by nested padding and parent labels rather than a second fill scale, so the status legend remains unambiguous. The two tiling methods keep the same hierarchy and values and only change how space is cut. Squarify aims for aspect ratios near squares, so large economies are easier to compare by area and country names fit more often. SliceDice alternates horizontal and vertical partitions by depth, which turns continents into stacked bands and areas into strips; that makes the Continent → Area → Country path easier to follow, but small countries become long, thin rectangles. Tooltips supply country, continent, area, GDP, and status when a cell is too small to label.
