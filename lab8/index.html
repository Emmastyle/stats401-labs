I used pages 10–400 and the PDF outline, so each passage still has a part, section, subsection, and page. Cover and table of contents are gone, along with page numbers, footnotes, headings, and leftover course-table rows. Duplicates were dropped. I did not strip stop words before embedding. Embeddings: all-MiniLM-L6-v2, 384-d, L2-normalized. UMAP: 15 neighbors, min_dist 0.15, cosine, random_state 401. Topics: 8-means on the original vectors, same seed.

5. Semantic Findings

Read from the three charts above, then from the map and the matrix.

What are the major semantic topics?

Eight colors on the map. The large ones are Global History, Culture, and Politics (194 passages), Policy, Health, and Society (176), Quantitative and Physical Sciences (158), and Language, Media, and Skills (157). Filter the map to any of those and most of the remaining points sit in Course Descriptions, which is also the tallest bar in “Passages by section.” The smaller colors are more like office language: University Identity and Study Away (133), Credit and Degree Requirements (121), Academic Standing and Withdrawal (82), Academic Calendar and Deadlines (40). That matches section 2. The frequency chart is almost all students and course. TF-IDF is more useful: chinese, prerequisite, credit are closer to the labels I actually used.

Which topics appear across multiple formal sections?

Look down the matrix columns. University Identity and Study Away is dark in 56 sections. Academic Standing and Withdrawal in 27, Credit and Degree Requirements in 23. Those are the same kinds of words TF-IDF picked up (duke, credit, academic). The science and history columns barely leave Course Descriptions and Majors. So a lot of the bulletin’s policy headings are repeating a few institutional topics, while the disciplinary material stays in Part 10.

Which formal sections contain the most semantically diverse content?

“Passages by section” is lopsided: Course Descriptions 631, Majors 78, then a long tail. The matrix row for Course Descriptions uses seven of eight topics. Majors uses six. Degree Requirements (30 passages, 5 topics) is the most mixed policy heading. The calendar is the opposite. It has 20 passages and they are all yellow. A long section in the bar chart is not automatically mixed, but the two longest ones happen to be.

Which passages from different sections are semantically similar?

Click a point and read the five nearest neighbors. The probation paragraph for 2022–2024 (p. 58) is almost the same as the one for 2025 and beyond (p. 60), similarity 0.994. Both say students have to clear probation to continue. A CR/NC note under Grading (p. 53) sits next to the study-away grade rule (p. 75), 0.958. The 40-credit cap is under Transfer of Work Taken Elsewhere (p. 39) and again under Office of Global Education and Study Away (p. 75), 0.923. Same sentence, two headings.

6. Design Description

Embeddings, topic labels, encodings, and why the two views sit together.

Passages were encoded with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384 dimensions, L2-normalized). Neighbors use cosine similarity on those vectors. Topics are 8-means on the same vectors, seed 401, not on the 2D map. UMAP is only for display: 15 neighbors, min_dist 0.15, cosine, same seed. Nearby points tend to mean similar things. The axes are not variables.

I named clusters after reading passages near each center and that cluster’s TF-IDF terms. The names are not copied from the table of contents. One group still mixes language courses, media, and PE. Those blurbs all sound like skill-practice notes, so the model puts them together. Section 2 is why I trusted TF-IDF more than raw counts: students and course are everywhere, while chinese and prerequisite actually differ across the book. The section bar chart is also why Course Descriptions eats the map. It is 631 of 1,061 passages, and that one row in the matrix uses seven topics.

On the map, position is UMAP, color is topic, size is word count. The matrix is section × topic; darker means more passages. Click a point for heading, page, text, five neighbors, and its cell. Click a cell to keep that pair. Search and the two menus dim the rest. That is how the probation wording and the 40-credit cap showed up under two different headings, which is the point of putting the formal structure next to the semantic one.
