I used pages 10–400 and the PDF outline, so each passage still has a part, section, subsection, and page. Cover and table of contents are gone, along with page numbers, footnotes, headings, and leftover course-table rows. Duplicates were dropped. I did not strip stop words before embedding. Embeddings: all-MiniLM-L6-v2, 384-d, L2-normalized. UMAP: 15 neighbors, min_dist 0.15, cosine, random_state 401. Topics: 8-means on the original vectors, same seed.

2. Corpus Overview

What words show up most often, and how long a typical passage is in each section.

Top meaningful terms

Counts after dropping English stop words. Students (1,110) and course (1,087) sit far above everything else, then a drop to duke, student, university, academic, chinese, prerequisite. The first two appear in almost every chapter, so they do not name a topic. The lower ones are a bit more local: prerequisite is mostly course listings, chinese shows up in language and identity passages. Frequency is still a blunt instrument. It just tells you this book is about students taking courses at DKU.

Average passage length by section

Mean word count for sections with at least eight passages. Changes in Status (108) and Grading (102) are the long policy blocks; Study Away is close behind (98). Those are the paragraphs you actually have to read. The calendar is the short one, about 26 words a passage, mostly dates. Course Descriptions sits at the corpus mean, 84, which is why most map points are mid-sized. Point size on the map is this same number, so the long policy rows here become the larger dots later.

5. Semantic Findings

Read from the two charts above, then from the map and the matrix.

What are the major semantic topics?

Eight colors on the map. The large ones are Global History, Culture, and Politics (194 passages), Policy, Health, and Society (176), Quantitative and Physical Sciences (158), and Language, Media, and Skills (157). Filter any of those and most of what remains is Course Descriptions. The smaller colors are more like office language: University Identity and Study Away (133), Credit and Degree Requirements (121), Academic Standing and Withdrawal (82), Academic Calendar and Deadlines (40). The two charts in section 2 already hint at that split. The term list is almost all students and course, which sit in every cluster. The length chart is more telling: policy sections run long, and the calendar is 26 words a passage and sits in one yellow cluster.

Which topics appear across multiple formal sections?

Look down the matrix columns. University Identity and Study Away is dark in 56 sections. Academic Standing and Withdrawal in 27, Credit and Degree Requirements in 23. Those are the policy-length rows on the length chart, not the 26-word calendar lines. The science and history columns barely leave Course Descriptions and Majors. So a lot of the bulletin’s policy headings are repeating a few institutional topics, while the disciplinary material stays in Part 10.

Which formal sections contain the most semantically diverse content?

Course Descriptions still uses seven of eight topic columns. Majors uses six. Among policy headings, Degree Requirements (5 topics) and Estimated Expenses (4) are the least uniform, and both are on the long side of the length chart (87 and 94 words). The calendar is the opposite: 20 passages, all yellow, 26 words each. A long section is not automatically mixed, but the short, formulaic one is the most uniform.

Which passages from different sections are semantically similar?

Click a point and read the five nearest neighbors. The probation paragraph for 2022–2024 (p. 58) is almost the same as the one for 2025 and beyond (p. 60), similarity 0.994. Both say students have to clear probation to continue. A CR/NC note under Grading (p. 53) sits next to the study-away grade rule (p. 75), 0.958. The 40-credit cap is under Transfer of Work Taken Elsewhere (p. 39) and again under Office of Global Education and Study Away (p. 75), 0.923. Same sentence, two headings.

6. Design Description

Embeddings, topic labels, encodings, and why the two views sit together.

Passages were encoded with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384 dimensions, L2-normalized). Neighbors use cosine similarity on those vectors. Topics are 8-means on the same vectors, seed 401, not on the 2D map. UMAP is only for display: 15 neighbors, min_dist 0.15, cosine, same seed. Nearby points tend to mean similar things. The axes are not variables.

I named clusters after reading passages near each center. The names are not copied from the table of contents. One group still mixes language courses, media, and PE. Those blurbs all sound like skill-practice notes, so the model puts them together. Section 2 is why I did not treat the word list as a topic map: students and course are everywhere. The length chart is why point size on the map is worth keeping. Policy sections such as Changes in Status average over 100 words. The calendar is 26. Those show up as large and small dots in different regions.

On the map, position is UMAP, color is topic, size is word count. The matrix is section × topic; darker means more passages. Click a point for heading, page, text, five neighbors, and its cell. Click a cell to keep that pair. Search and the two menus dim the rest. That is how the probation wording and the 40-credit cap showed up under two different headings, which is the point of putting the formal structure next to the semantic one.
