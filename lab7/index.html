Task 11. Static vs. Animated Temporal Visualization

The line chart maps time to space, so the whole 180-day trend is visible at once: winter-to-summer warming in Tokyo and New York, London’s cooler spring, and shared peaks after a metric switch. Animation maps data time to playback time. The marker is useful for change at a single moment, but comparing January with June then depends on memory. The design on this page therefore keeps the overview line, the moving marker, and the time controls together rather than relying on animation alone.

Part E. Temporal Network Questions

Answers come from the encodings above: link count, node size, regional position, fade in/out, and the daily summary.

Question Encoding / interaction Observation from the visualization
1. How does overall network connectivity change across the 60 days? Number of visible links; Active companies / Active links / Total value in the summary; Play across all 60 days Connectivity is uneven at first, then denser. Early days swing between sparse frames of three links and busier frames of seven or eight. After about day 21 the empty-looking frames disappear: most days show five to seven links. From day 41 onward the graph is often full. Several late days light up all 12 companies, and the total-value readout climbs from roughly $40k–$115k early to peaks near $200k (for example day 51). The network becomes more connected, not less, over the 60 days.
2. Which companies become more central or commercially active over time? Node size (current volume); stronger outline when active; tooltips Fusion Electronics (Technology, Asia) is the persistent hub: it stays large through the middle third of the timeline. Ion Systems and Cedar Retail are modest early, then swell in the last 20 days, when new cross-regional ties attach to them. Evergreen Foods stays the smallest node on almost every frame. BlueRiver Logistics and Harbor Shipping remain steadily mid-sized rather than taking over. Activity therefore shifts from Fusion-centered mid-period trade toward a late mix led by Ion and Cedar, with Fusion still present.
3. Are there periods when the network consists of relatively separate clusters? Regional forces (Asia left, Europe center, North America right); absence of long cross-region links; Cross-regional count Yes, especially on days 1, 7, 13, and 19. Those frames show three same-region pairs and a cross-regional count of 0, so the graph reads as three disconnected regional dyads rather than one component. Mid-period days still have a few within-region bundles, but long links already bridge the three columns. Late days rarely look clustered: almost every frame has several links crossing from Asia to North America or Europe.
4. Which commercial relationships appear, disappear, or become stronger over time? Link fade in/out; link width (amount); link color (type); pause and scrub Some ties never leave the stage: Cedar–Evergreen (goods, both North America), Ion–Lumen (services), Apex–Fusion (components), and Delta–Fusion (thick components links) recur across the 60 days. Early-only ties such as BlueRiver–Delta, Cedar–Fusion, and Harbor–Keystone fade out after day 20. Fusion–Granite and Granite–Harbor last into the middle third and then vanish. After day 41, new thick links appear around Cedar (to Granite, Keystone, and Harbor) and around Ion (to Delta, Apex, and Fusion). Strength is visible as width: Delta–Fusion is among the heaviest recurring edges.
5. Does the network become more or less cross-regional over time? Long links that cross the three regional columns; Cross-regional summary; region shape More cross-regional. Early and middle days often show about two-thirds of links crossing regions, and a few early days show none. In the last 20 days the cross-regional share rises (often six or seven of nine or ten links), and the layout is dominated by long edges between the Asia, Europe, and North America columns rather than by short within-region chords. The late network is more mixed geographically, not more local.
